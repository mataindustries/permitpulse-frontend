# The Paper Trail Loop

The Paper Trail Loop is PermitPulse’s responsible operating mechanism:

> Customer question → source search → verification → research brief → anonymized reusable lesson → jurisdiction playbook update → content asset → measured customer response → improved checklist → reverify on the next case

It is a research and operations loop, not an autonomous training claim.

## Operating stages

1. **Frame the customer question.** Convert the request into material, decision-linked questions.
2. **Search current sources.** Start with primary official agency and public-record sources; record URLs, retrieval dates, jurisdiction, identifiers used, and access limits.
3. **Verify.** Corroborate material claims where appropriate. Label each conclusion verified fact, reasonable inference, or unknown. Log conflicts rather than resolving them by assumption.
4. **Brief.** Deliver a concise source-backed summary, timeline, conflicts and gaps, unresolved questions, and next calls.
5. **Extract an anonymized lesson.** Retain reusable patterns only when customer and property identity cannot be reconstructed.
6. **Update the jurisdiction playbook.** Improve source maps, query patterns, checklists, source-reliability notes, access requirements, and recurring failure patterns.
7. **Create content only when evidence supports it.** Use a source packet and the applicable editorial review. Do not turn private work into public proof by default.
8. **Measure response.** Use aggregate, non-PII events to learn which questions and explanations lead to sample views and research-intake actions.
9. **Improve the checklist.** Record useful objection language, missed search paths, and quality-control changes.
10. **Reverify.** Reopen current primary sources for every new case and before updating a public claim.

## Privacy rules

Never place the following in reusable playbooks, source packets intended for publication, analytics events, or content calendars:

- customer names, emails, phone numbers, or company identifiers;
- exact customer addresses or parcel identifiers;
- free-text intake descriptions;
- private correspondence or documents;
- source URLs whose query string exposes a private address or record identifier;
- screenshots with names, addresses, parcel numbers, account details, barcodes, or metadata;
- a combination of “anonymized” details that could reasonably reidentify a private person or property.

Keep case evidence in the case-specific workspace. Reusable notes should describe the jurisdiction, source route, query pattern, reliability issue, or general failure pattern without case identifiers.

Analytics may include content ID, content lane, page path, CTA location, sanitized destination path, form type, and event name. It must not include form values or URL query strings.

## Currency and stale-information controls

Each reusable source note and public source packet must have:

- jurisdiction and source owner;
- canonical source URL;
- retrieved/last-verified date;
- the claim the source supports;
- expected volatility: high, medium, or low;
- next-review trigger;
- known access or reliability limit.

Mark a note **stale** when any of these occurs:

- its explicit review date arrives;
- the source returns an error, redirects materially, or changes owner/domain;
- the agency announces a rule, system, fee, form, or workflow change;
- a new case conflicts with the note;
- the note is used for a new customer conclusion after 90 days without a more specific review cadence;
- a source has no visible effective date and the claim affects a costly decision.

A stale note may help locate the next source. It may not support a new conclusion until reverified.

## Reverification checklist

Before using a prior lesson on a new case:

1. Open the current primary source directly.
2. Confirm jurisdiction, effective date, and applicability.
3. Capture the new retrieval date.
4. Compare the current language or record with the stored note.
5. Update or retire the note if it changed.
6. Re-run the case-specific search; do not reuse another property’s conclusion.
7. Keep the source and any material limit beside the client-facing claim.

## Quality and escalation

Escalate a conclusion to “unknown—confirm with agency/professional” when sources conflict, the record cannot be authenticated, the relevant document is restricted or unavailable, the jurisdiction is uncertain, or the question requires legal or licensed judgment.

A better checklist improves speed and consistency. It never removes the duty to verify.
