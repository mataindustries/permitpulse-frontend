# Permit Nightmares editorial and legal-safety standard

**Franchise line:** Permit Nightmares — true bureaucracy, useful lessons.

Permit Nightmares may use suspense, scene-setting, withheld resolution, and reveal pacing. The entertainment layer never outruns the evidence. The objective is a memorable, useful explanation of a permitting failure pattern—not fear, blame, or spectacle.

## Allowed source bases

A published story must use one of:

1. a redacted real case with documented consent or a defensible publication basis and a completed reidentification review;
2. verified public institutional records, such as an official directive, audit, court decision, hearing record, or agency report;
3. a composite built from verified recurring patterns and labeled **COMPOSITE** in the title area and every derivative.

If none is available, keep the packet as an internal draft. Never invent an event and present it as real.

## Non-negotiable rules

- Never expose a private homeowner, applicant, address, parcel number, private contact detail, or sensitive personal information.
- Never imply criminal conduct, fraud, corruption, or misconduct without directly relevant authoritative adjudicated evidence.
- Never use “nightmare” as proof that a person, company, department, or decision was at fault.
- Distinguish **verified fact**, **reasonable inference**, and **unknown** in the packet, article, script, Shorts, and social copy.
- Cite primary official sources beside material factual claims.
- State the last-verified date and whether the source documents a completed event, a stated policy, or a proposed/ordered reform.
- Label redactions and composites prominently.
- Do not provide legal advice or a property-specific official conclusion.
- End with the evidence-backed failure pattern, useful lesson, and next action.
- Remove suspense language if it would make a qualified or uncertain claim sound established.

## Required prepublication header

Every Permit Nightmare must record:

- case basis: redacted real case / institutional public record / composite;
- publication status: internal draft / legal-editorial review / approved for scheduling;
- jurisdiction;
- last verified;
- primary source list;
- private-person or property exposure: none / redacted, with review notes;
- allegation check;
- fact/inference/unknown check;
- composite label check;
- CTA and service-boundary check.

## Narrative structure

1. **Cold open:** the decision and the confusing bureaucratic condition, with no invented detail.
2. **The paper trail:** dated verified facts and sources.
3. **The turn:** the record conflict, dependency, or evidence gap.
4. **What can be inferred:** reasoning stated as inference, with limits.
5. **What remains unknown:** missing evidence and responsible confirmation route.
6. **Recurring failure pattern:** the generalizable operational problem.
7. **Useful lesson:** what the audience should document, ask, or verify next.
8. **Contextual CTA:** Permit Deep Research for a real address; no fear-based pressure.

## Language checks

Prefer:

- “the record shows”;
- “the directive orders”;
- “the reviewed source does not establish”;
- “a reasonable inference is”;
- “this remains unknown”;
- “confirm with the responsible agency or professional.”

Avoid unless authoritative evidence directly supports it:

- “they hid,” “illegal,” “scam,” “corrupt,” “fraud,” “cover-up,” “guilty,” “caught,” “victim,” “the agency failed,” or “the owner knew.”

## Starter format decision

The first publishable Permit Nightmare, “Nine departments, one paper trail,” is based only on Los Angeles Executive Directive No. 19. It uses no private case or address, alleges no misconduct, and explicitly distinguishes the directive’s ordered reforms from completed implementation.
