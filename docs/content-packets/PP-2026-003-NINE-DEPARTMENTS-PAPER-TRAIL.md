## Evidence register

### Verified facts

- The directive directly addresses nine named departments: Building and Safety, City Planning, Public Works Bureau of Engineering, Public Works Bureau of Contract Administration, Fire, Housing, Sanitation, Transportation, and Water and Power, with other departments included as needed.
- It directs coordinated work on simultaneous electronic plan-review access and proactive review workflows.
- It calls for expanded online permits and virtual inspections.
- It directs mapping and redesign of development-service workstreams and action to reduce or simplify clearances.
- These statements describe ordered reform; the source is not a completion report.

### Reasonable inference

- Inference: a single customer-visible status may describe only part of the project’s cross-department dependency chain.
- Reasoning: the directive expressly calls for shared access, coordinated review, mapped workstreams, and clearance simplification across multiple departments.
- What would confirm or overturn it: current department-specific records, written agency responses, and implementation reporting for the particular project and time period.

### Unknowns

- Which directive measures are complete as of publication.
- How a particular application is routed.
- Whether any named department caused a particular delay.
- Whether a displayed status is current or complete.
- Confirmation route: current agency records and responses; official implementation reporting; responsible department and qualified project professionals.

## Customer value

Recurring failure pattern: one visible status gets asked to explain a multi-owner process.  
Useful takeaway: create a dependency ledger with workflow owner, source, retrieval date, displayed status, open dependency, responsible confirmer, and next question.  
Next action: map each material question to its responsible department or professional before drawing a project-level conclusion.  
Relevant Permit Deep Research use case: an address has multiple permit, planning, clearance, utility, or inspection records that do not form a clear sequence.
