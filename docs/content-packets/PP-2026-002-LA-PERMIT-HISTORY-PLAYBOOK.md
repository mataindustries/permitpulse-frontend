## Evidence register

### Verified facts

- The City’s official page provides multiple online research routes rather than one universal property conclusion.
- The page describes additional retrieval through the Records Section for unavailable online documents.
- Some plan copies have additional ownership and authorization requirements.

### Reasonable inference

- Inference: a decision-ready history requires a defined customer question, multiple applicable official routes, dated observations, and explicit gaps.
- Reasoning: a single result cannot establish documents or questions that the official page routes elsewhere or qualifies.
- What would confirm or overturn it: the specific jurisdiction, official property results, responsive records, and agency/professional confirmations for the property.

### Unknowns

- The property’s correct permitting jurisdiction until checked.
- Whether the displayed record set is complete.
- What a status means for current legality, completion, scope, or proposed work.
- Confirmation route: responsible agency and the appropriate licensed or title professional when the question exceeds public-record research.

## Derivatives 3–5: Shorts

### Short 1 — Start with the decision

Hook: “Do not start permit research with the portal.”

Script: “Start with the decision. If you are bidding work, ask which documented scope, open item, or missing final could change the price. If you are buying, ask which record conflict could change diligence. Then open the current official routes. A focused question tells you which records matter; otherwise you collect screenshots without knowing what they prove.”

On-screen source note: Research method; official routes linked in the full playbook.  
CTA: Read the Paper Trail Playbook.

### Short 2 — Fact, inference, unknown

Hook: “A permit entry can be real and your conclusion can still be wrong.”

Script: “Label the entry first: verified fact—this is what the dated official source displays. Then label your interpretation: reasonable inference—supported, but not established. Finally list what remains unknown. This stops a status label from becoming a code, legality, completion, or feasibility conclusion the source never made.”

On-screen source note: PermitPulse evidence labels; verify property conclusions with agency/professionals.  
CTA: Research an address.

### Short 3 — Reverify

Hook: “Your old permit note is a map, not current evidence.”

Script: “Agency pages, portals, workflows, and records change. A prior note can help you find the source again, but before a new bid or brief, reopen the current primary source, capture a new retrieval date, and compare it with the old note. If the page changed or the new property conflicts with the playbook, mark the note stale and update it.”

On-screen source note: Reverify current primary sources.  
CTA: See the full playbook.
