## Evidence register

### Verified facts

- The official page points users to online permit and inspection reports, ZIMAS, and Atlas/NavigateLA.
- It describes records that may be available, including permits, certificates of occupancy, range files, plot plans, geology and soils materials, affidavits, and plans.
- It directs users to the Records Section for documents that are not available online.
- It states that extra documentation and authorization can apply to copies of plans.

### Reasonable inference

- Inference: an online search can be a useful first layer without being a complete property conclusion.
- Reasoning: the same official source distinguishes online routes from additional record retrieval and names record types with access requirements.
- What would confirm or overturn it: a case-specific search, Records Section response, and any applicable agency or property documents.

Recurring failure pattern: “Not visible online” gets converted into “does not exist.”  
Useful takeaway: record three states—found and reviewed; official route checked but not found online; additional retrieval required.

### Short 2 — Three evidence states

Hook: “One label can prevent a costly permit assumption.”

Script: “For every material property record, use one of three states: found and reviewed; official route checked but not found online; or additional retrieval required. The second state matters. A blank result does not tell you whether the record never existed, sits in another system, or requires a records request. Keep the retrieval date beside the label, then ask the responsible agency the next narrow question.”

On-screen source note: LA DBS official building-record routes; verified 2026-08-22.

### Short 3 — Plans are different

Hook: “Why can’t every plan set be downloaded like a permit entry?”

Script: “The City’s building-record page explains that copies of plans can involve ownership documentation and authorization requirements. That means a plan can be relevant and still not be available through an ordinary public search. Do not promise a document before checking the current access rules. Mark the requirement, identify who can authorize access, and keep that limitation in the brief.”
